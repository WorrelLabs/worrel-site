01 / THE GAP

The world measures people, but can't see all of them.

A wrong match is an economic cost.

Degrees, certificates, titles, references and scores all matter. On their own, they don't tell us where a person can create the most value.

Potential that stays hidden.

04 / THE START

Where it starts

Built first for Türkiye's hospitality workforce.

Hotels, restaurants and cafés run on people. Worrel starts where reliability is hardest to see and matters most.

01
Discover Free for people

Job matching by swipe, plus QR storefront applications. Free for people, permanently.

↗
02
WRS Reliability score

Worrel Reliability Score, 400–950, built from peer validation: Circles, Skill Votes and Vouching. Validators are anonymous, and nobody picks their own.

↗
03
DBA Digital commitment agreement

A time-bound commitment between employer and employee that opens mutual Signal. Built for existing employment relationships only.

↗
04
Thermo For multi-branch teams

Employee and customer sentiment across locations, as live heat maps for restaurant chains and other multi-branch businesses.

↗
05
Light One scenario a day

An everyday scenario each day builds a behavior profile. Tags describe styles, never good or bad, and only validation from competent people brings a tendency forward.

05 / THE REFUSALS

What we won't do is part of the vision.

The architecture has a conscience.

A strong infrastructure says what it does — and what it refuses to do.

01 / 06

No “best person” rankings

One ranking, detached from context, never explains a person.

02 / 06

No deciding for people

Worrel raises the quality of information. The decision stays human.

03 / 06

No single score as the whole person

Every signal shows one dimension, never all of someone.

04 / 06

No raw data turned into gossip

Insights come as patterns, trends and early warnings, once there is enough density.

05 / 06

No hidden manipulation

Anonymity, explainability and abuse resistance are part of the architecture.

06 / 06

No technology for its own sake

AI and data matter only when they help people understand themselves and the world.
